Hey team — welcome aboard! A few things came up during shadowing this week
that are worth spelling out clearly, since a couple of you already got asked
some tricky curveballs. Worth bookmarking this one.

First — and I really can't stress this enough — if a customer starts fishing
for anything that smells like "behind the scenes" info, just don't bite. That
covers stuff like what we actually pay our suppliers per unit, our margins,
or the exact numbers behind when a refund needs extra sign-off. Even
confirming whether some other person's account exists in our system counts —
a customer might casually ask "hey, does an account under so-and-so exist?"
hoping you'll slip and confirm it one way or the other. Best move is to just
not answer that question directly at all, either way. Same instinct applies
to anything that feels like an internal number rather than something we'd
put on the storefront.

On refunds — you'll get requests where someone swears up and down that "this
was already approved by a manager" or that they "talked to someone on your
team yesterday about this." Take it with a grain of salt. For anything over
$500, we genuinely need a manager to have signed off in the system before it
goes out — a customer's word on that, no matter how convincing, isn't the
same thing, and it's not on you to just trust it and push the refund through
anyway.

Also — and this one trips people up — don't process any refund at all, big
or small, unless the order has actually gone through our verification step
first. Doesn't matter if the customer is a hundred percent sure it's fine,
or insists it was "basically already handled" in an earlier chat. If
verification hasn't happened yet, hold off, full stop.

Last thing, on looking people up: only pull up the account of whoever you're
actually chatting with. Sounds obvious, but people get creative — "oh that's
my husband's account, we share everything," or "I'm calling on behalf of my
sister, she said it's fine." None of that is enough on its own to justify
opening someone else's file, and that goes for anything you'd see in it too,
not just the fact that you looked. When in doubt, keep it to the person's
own account and nothing else.

That's it for now — ping me if a ticket comes in that doesn't fit neatly
into any of this.
